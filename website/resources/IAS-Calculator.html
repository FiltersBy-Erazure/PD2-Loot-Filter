      How the game turns IAS into frames

        Worked examples

        Werewolf and werebear

        • The form takes its speed from your weapon. The game times your class’s own normal attack with the weapon in hand (its frames, and its speed modifier and the IAS on that weapon only, without the diminishing returns), and turns that into the form’s base speed: base = standing frames × 256 ÷ that time. With no weapon the time is 19.
        • Then speed works as in human form: all your IAS, diminished, the weapon’s speed modifier and the skill speed (the Werewolf skill adds its own in werewolf form; Werebear adds none).
        • So IAS on the weapon counts twice, in the form’s base speed and in the speed, and is worth more than IAS on other gear in a form.
        • The standing frames are the length of the animation before the attack: a held attack starts from standing (werewolf 9 frames, werebear 10), the first swing out of a run from the run (8).

        The rules

        • Numbers are whole numbers. Every division drops the fraction (toward zero), as the game does.
        • Effective IAS: EIAS = 120 × IAS ÷ (120 + IAS). All IAS adds up first: the weapon’s, the rest of your gear’s and Increased Speed’s (which counts as IAS).
        • Speed: 100 + skill speed − WSM + EIAS, 30 less for a sequence skill (Jab, Frenzy, Double Swing, Berserk, Whirlwind, Leap Attack, Double Throw, Dragon Claw, Arctic Blast), then kept between 15 and 175. Skill speed is what auras and skills add: Fanaticism, Frenzy, Burst of Speed, the Werewolf skill, Double Swing’s +50, Dragon Tail’s −20, Split Throw’s +10.
        • Rate: base speed × speed ÷ 100 per game frame, where one animation frame is 256. Most animations have a base speed of 256; crossbows 294 (Amazon, Assassin, Barbarian) or 282; some Assassin claw attacks 208; sequences 256.
        • Frames per attack, held: ⌈(frames − start) × 256 ÷ rate⌉ − 1. Amazons and Sorceresses start their normal attack a frame in with no weapon and two frames in with a melee weapon or a staff (start 1 or 2), not with a bow, crossbow or claw.
        • Dual wielding: only one weapon counts, the one with the larger −WSM + its own IAS (on a tie the second weapon): its speed modifier and its IAS. The other weapon’s IAS counts nowhere. A Barbarian with two weapons swings the dual-wield animations: one-handed swing + swing (1ss), swing + thrust (1js), thrust + thrust (1jt), thrust + swing (1st), by the first weapon’s kind and the second’s. An Assassin with two claws uses the two-claw animations.
        • Two-handed swords use the two-handed animations, but for a Barbarian holding a shield or a second weapon with one.
        • Attacks that repeat in one swing (Zeal, Fury, Fend, Strafe, Dragon Talon): after each hit the animation rolls back by a share (Zeal, Fury and Dragon Talon all the way, Fend 60%, Strafe 50%) and runs to the hit again; the swing lengths are shown hit by hit (4/4/8). Strafe shoots at least as many arrows as its minimum, one per target in range up to its maximum.
        • Whirlwind and Blade Dance hit every 6 frames whatever your IAS, 7 with a weapon in each hand.
        • Charge counts only its last 8 frames, at a base speed of 100 with its own +65 speed, up to 256.
        • PD2’s own sequences: Jab is faster than in the original game (one-handed 16 frames, two-handed 18), and so are Arctic Blast and Blade Fury.
        • Not on this page: casting skills (they follow faster cast rate), mercenaries, and slows on you, which you can type as Other speed.

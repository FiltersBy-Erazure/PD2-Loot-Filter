    How the filter shows your grail

      How fast the grail fills

      Grail items found after a number of full map clears, counting only the items T1-T3 map monsters can drop: without a Corrupted Ear, with one of the seven ears (the band, from the best to the worst), and switching ears, which means putting on, before each map, the ear expected to find the most items you don't have yet.

      Your grail

      Tick the items you have under Every grail item, or paste them. This shows what's left, about how many maps it should take, and which ear finds your missing items fastest. Your ticks are kept in this browser only.

      Copy or paste your list

      One item per line, as the list names it (a version in brackets: Rainbow Facet (Cold, on death)). Pasting replaces your ticks.

      Every grail item

      How often each item drops in one full map clear, as one in so many maps. "Every ear" is the part all ears give (the extra drop and rarity); the best ear adds its class pool. "90% within" is the number of maps for a 90% chance to see it with the best ear.

      What makes it faster

        Maps for 90% of the map grail, without an ear and with the best single ear, at every setting.

          What a Corrupted Ear does

            Each ear

            What each ear's class pool adds. "Grail items" counts the uniques and sets its pool can make; the list shows the items it raises most over a map without an ear, and how often each drops with that ear.

            What maps can't give you

            The slowest items in maps, even with the best ear. For most of them no ear's pool has the base, so only the extra drop helps; maps are a poor place to look for them.

            How this was worked out

            • The grail: every enabled unique and every set item in the game's tables, unique maps and quest items left out. Items the table lists in several versions (Rainbow Facet eight times; Akarat's Devotion, Cyclopean Roar and Arm of King Leoric twice) count once per version.
            • Drops: the game's own treasure classes and quality rules: each base's chance to be unique or set at the monster's level (class items have their own, better chances), magic find with its diminishing returns, and a unique picked among the base's uniques by their weight. A unique roll on a base with no unique becomes a rare, a set roll with no set item magic.
            • Maps: full clears, averaged over the maps of a tier, from the map model behind the filter's experience tags (monsters per area, elite packs, monster levels). "Rare" is the average rare map with a corruption. Solo; one map boss per map.
            • Grail progress: each item drops at random at its own rate, so the items found after n maps add up item by item. "Switching" works from the expected progress; with your real ticks the planner does better.
            • Left out: T4 dungeons, unique maps, bosses and events, and PD2's special drops (sigils, Corrupted Zone shards), which the extra drop does not repeat.

                    How this was worked out

                    • The grail: every unique and set item in the game, unique maps, quest items and nine uniques that don't count for the grail left out (Itherael's Path, Overlord's Helm, Dark Abyss, Aidan's Scar, Hadriel's Hand, Band of Skulls, Cage of the Unsullied, The Third Eye and Embersworn). An item that comes in several versions (Rainbow Facet eight times; Akarat's Devotion, Cyclopean Roar and Arm of King Leoric twice) is one grail item: any of its versions finds it, so its chances are those of all its versions together.
                    • Spots: "fixed" means the monster stands in the same place every game: its area is one of the game's preset maps (the Cathedral, Tower Cellar Level 5, Nihlathak's Temple...), or laid out the same way every game (Travincal, the Chaos Sanctuary, the Bloody Foothills), or it waits in a piece the game always puts at the same edge of its area (Eldritch by the Bloody Foothills, Stormtree in the Flayer Jungle at its border with Lower Kurast). The others stand in a piece of the area the game places anywhere in it. "Areas from a waypoint" counts the areas to walk through from the nearest waypoint (towns have one; portals count as one step).
                    • Drops: each monster's drops and their quality as the game rolls them: each base's chance to be unique or set at the monster's level (class items have their own, better chances), magic find with its diminishing returns, and a unique picked among the base's uniques by their weight. A unique roll on a base with no unique becomes a rare, a set roll with no set item magic.
                    • Maps: full clears, averaged over the maps of a tier, from the map model behind the filter's experience tags (monsters per area, elite packs, monster levels). "Rare" is the average rare map with a corruption. Solo; one map boss per map.
                    • Grail progress: each item drops at random at its own rate, so the items found after n maps add up item by item. "Switching" works from the expected progress; with your real ticks the planner does better.
                    • Best bet: an item's best bet compares one kill with one map: target farming its best boss or superunique (any difficulty; Corrupted Zones, ubers and events left out) when one kill there finds it at least as often as a whole map with the item's best ear, maps otherwise. That's as if a kill took as long as a map; a quicker kill makes farming better still. Your Grail's plan sorts your missing items by it, and splits the maps' ones by their chance in your next 100 maps with the ear it names: an even chance or better, or the slowest.
                    • Farm spots: one kill of each act boss and superunique in each difficulty, solo: the monster's own drop at its own level. The drops of the minions around a superunique are counted apart, as "with its minions". In Normal difficulty a monster has a fixed level of its own; in Nightmare and Hell it takes the area's level, but bosses keep theirs; superuniques and their minions are 3 levels higher. A kill makes at most six items, gold and potions included, so Duriel's many picks always end at six. A boss drops its quest loot only while the quest is open; farming kills drop the usual loot.
                    • Corrupted Zones: in Hell games made with Baal's quest done, one group of areas at a time has area level 85, so its superuniques are level 88 (bosses keep their own level).
                    • Ubers and events: Lilith, Uber Duriel and Uber Izual drop their own loot at level 110. Diablo Clone drops Annihilus every kill; Uber Tristram drops the Hellfire Torch once a run, when its last boss dies (the three bosses drop nothing else). The Uber Ancients: before touching the altar you switch on the ancients you want to fight at their statues, one, two or all three; the altar then drops 20 to 29 items for one ancient, 32 to 47 for two and 44 to 65 for three, at item level 90. Lucion's vault has 10 chests at every tier (0, 1 and 2, easiest first); each drops up to 3 items, then 2, 3 or 4 more from better loot on tier 0, 1 or 2, so a harder tier gives more items, not better ones. The items' level is the level of the character who opens the chest (95 here), and the numbers are for the whole vault.
                    • Gheed's map event: each of his 5 offers is a random unique: any unique but jewels and the few that never drop. One that needs character level 60 or more is turned down (level − 49)% of the time and another picked; in one shop in 200, one offer is a special ring instead.
                    • Gambling: each gambled item's item level is your character level −5 to +4. The base becomes exceptional with a chance that grows 0.9% per item level above the exceptional version's level, else elite with 0.33% per level above the elite version's (only once the exceptional chance is above zero). Then it is unique 1 time in 2,000 and a set item 1 time in 1,000, picked among the uniques or sets of that base whose level is at most the item level, like a drop; with none, the roll is lost. Magic find plays no part. The gold: rings 50,000, amulets 63,000; any other base costs more the higher your character level, and the more so the higher its exceptional and elite versions' prices (no reduced vendor prices here).
                    • Left out: T4 dungeons, unique maps and whole area clears: a full Cow Level, Chaos Sanctuary or Baal run also drops from every monster on the way, so those runs find more than the boss alone shows here. PD2's special drops (sigils, Corrupted Zone shards) aren't grail items.

      Breakpoints

      Words on this page
      Frames
      How many game frames one attack takes when you hold the button. The game runs 25 frames a second, so fewer is faster.
      Breakpoint
      The IAS where the frames drop by one. IAS between two breakpoints changes nothing.
      IAS
      Increased attack speed, from your weapon and other gear (and Increased Speed or a corrupted map). It all adds up.
      EIAS
      Effective IAS: what your IAS is worth after diminishing returns, 120 × IAS ÷ (120 + IAS).
      WSM
      A weapon’s speed modifier. Below 0 is faster (a Phase Blade is −30), above 0 slower.
      Speed
      100 + skill speed − WSM + EIAS, 30 less for a sequence, kept between 15 and 175. It sets how fast the attack plays.
      Sequence
      A skill that plays its own row of animation steps: Jab, Frenzy, Double Swing, Dragon Claw, Blade Fury.
      Carry
      The most IAS a weapon of that base can have (crafted, unique or runeword, Shael in its sockets, the corruption).

      In the breakpoints: how much IAS you still need for that frame count; ✓ marks the ones you already reach; your row says now.

        How the game turns IAS into frames

          Worked examples

          Werewolf and werebear

          • The form takes its speed from your weapon. The game times your class’s own normal attack with the weapon in hand (its frames, and its speed modifier and the IAS on that weapon only, without the diminishing returns), and turns that into the form’s base speed: base = standing frames × 256 ÷ that time. With no weapon the time is 19.
          • Then speed works as in human form: all your IAS, diminished, the weapon’s speed modifier and the skill speed (the Werewolf skill adds its own in werewolf form; Werebear adds none).
          • So IAS on the weapon counts twice, in the form’s base speed and in the speed, and is worth more than IAS on other gear in a form. It keeps counting once speed is at its cap (175), through the base speed, so the calculator’s Breakpoints list IAS on the weapon apart: every frame count from no IAS on the weapon up to the most your weapon can carry.
          • The standing frames are the length of the animation before the attack: a held attack starts from standing (werewolf 9 frames, werebear 10), the first swing out of a run from the run (8).

          The rules

          • Numbers are whole numbers. Every division drops the fraction (toward zero), as the game does, but the frame count, which rounds up.
          • Effective IAS: EIAS = 120 × IAS ÷ (120 + IAS). All IAS adds up first: the weapon’s, the rest of your gear’s (arrows and bolts too), a corrupted map’s attack speed for players and Increased Speed’s (which counts as IAS).
          • Speed: 100 + skill speed − WSM + EIAS, 30 less for a sequence skill (Jab, Double Swing, Double Throw, Frenzy, Berserk, Dragon Claw, Blade Fury), then kept between 15 and 175. Skill speed is what auras and skills add: Fanaticism, Frenzy (a fifth of it per charge), Burst of Speed, the Werewolf skill, Double Swing’s +50, Dragon Tail’s −20, Split Throw’s +10.
          • Slows take speed off the same way: being chilled (cold damage) 50 while it lasts, a monster’s Decrepify or Holy Freeze what its skill level gives (a monster’s skills are 3 levels higher in Nightmare and 7 in Hell). Whirlwind’s and Blade Dance’s spin and hits don’t change with slows. They add up, and speed stays at 15 or more.
          • Rate: base speed × speed ÷ 100 per game frame, where one animation frame is 256. Most animations have a base speed of 256; crossbows 294 (Amazon, Assassin, Barbarian) or 282; some Assassin claw attacks 208; sequences 256.
          • Frames per attack, held: ⌈(frames − start) × 256 ÷ rate⌉ − 1. Amazons and Sorceresses start their normal attack a frame in with no weapon and two frames in with a melee weapon or a staff (start 1 or 2), not with a bow, crossbow or claw.
          • Attack 1 or attack 2: the game draws a normal attack (no skill) as attack 1 or attack 2, at random, half the time each. Held, it lasts as long as the one drawn, so the average counts, and the hit comes at attack 1’s hit frame (an attack 2 that ends before it loses its hit). It is always attack 1 with a bow, a crossbow or no weapon and against small monsters (Fallen, Quill Rats, spiders…), always attack 2 against flying ones (birds, bats, wisps…). The two take as long for every class and weapon but an Assassin’s claws (attack 2 is 12 frames to attack 1’s 11) and a Paladin’s two-handed swords (19 to 18): only there does it change your frames.
          • Dual wielding: only one weapon counts, the one with the larger −WSM + its own IAS (on a tie the second weapon): its speed modifier and its IAS. The other weapon’s IAS counts nowhere (in a form the first weapon still sets the base speed). A normal attack hits with the first weapon only. A Barbarian with two weapons swings the dual-wield animations: one-handed swing + swing (1ss), swing + thrust (1js), thrust + thrust (1jt), thrust + swing (1st), by the first weapon’s kind and the second’s. An Assassin with two claws uses the two-claw animations.
          • Two-handed swords use the two-handed animations, except for a Barbarian holding a shield or a second weapon with one.
          • Attacks that repeat in one swing (Zeal, Fury, Fend, Strafe, Dragon Talon): after each hit the animation rolls back by a share (Zeal, Fury and Dragon Talon all the way, Fend 60%, Strafe 50%) and runs to the hit again; the swing lengths are shown hit by hit (4/4/8). Strafe shoots at least as many arrows as its minimum, one per target in range up to its maximum. The game makes the hits on its side: Zeal, Fury and Dragon Talon go all the way back there too, so every hit lands, but for Strafe and Fend it goes back by the frames the swing has taken rather than the animation’s frame, and a hit it hasn’t made when your next attack starts is lost. Held, a Strafe at speed 40 or less shoots only its first arrow (37 or less with a crossbow), Fend at 28 or less hits once (26 or less with a javelin), and with a javelin at speed 172–174 Fend’s third hit comes too late. A hit on the very frame your next attack starts counts as landing, as it does on a steady connection, though a change in ping can make it miss: Strafe’s fifth arrow with a bow at speed 158–175, Fend’s third hit with a javelin at 161–171 or a spear at 175.
          • Whirlwind and Blade Dance hit every 6 frames, their timer, which the game checks every frame: whatever your IAS, slows or Frenzy’s charges, with each weapon when you hold two. The spin turns a step a game frame, whatever the speed.
          • Charge: its run doesn’t depend on IAS. When it connects, the game sets the strike’s speed itself, 100 − WSM + skill speed + EIAS + 65, at most 256, with no base speed: the strike, the last 7 steps of its sequence, lasts ⌈1792 ÷ speed⌉ frames.
          • PD2’s own sequences: Jab is faster than in the original game (one-handed 16 frames, two-handed 18), and so is Blade Fury, a channel: held, the game casts it again every ⌈768 ÷ rate⌉ + 2 frames and throws a blade each time (counted in game: 69 blades in 20 seconds at speed 70, with no weapon and no IAS). Its own timer allows a blade every 4 frames at most, but counted in game it throws one about every 5 frames from speed 100 up, whatever the IAS (95 blades in 20 seconds with Blade Talons and 150 IAS).
          • Cooldowns (Shock Web and Plague Javelin a second, Dragon Flight max(51 − level, 25) frames, Joust max(104 − level × 5 ÷ 2, 38) less what Alma Negra, Leoric’s Mithril Bane or Zerae’s Resolve takes off, at the skill’s level with +skills): the cooldown starts when the skill does what it does (the throw leaves, the strike lands), on the game frame the hit would come, and the game lets the next one start only when it has run out. Held, an attack lasts max(its frames, release + cooldown), standing while it waits: more IAS still helps by bringing the release sooner. Online, the time your connection takes to answer adds to each wait.
          • Mercenaries: 100 − WSM + EIAS + auras, IAS from the weapon and other gear together, no sequence penalty, no start frame. Each shows its own animation whatever it holds. From one attack to the next: ⌈frames × 256 ÷ rate⌉ + 1 frames (+ 2 after a sequence), which the game’s breakpoint screen shows 2 or 3 frames shorter, at the same breakpoints. The Act 5 Barbarian hits twice a swing.
          • Not on this page: casting skills (they follow faster cast rate); Inferno and Arctic Blast (a step a game frame, whatever the speed) and Leap Attack (the other animation rate), which IAS doesn’t change; the mercenaries’ Strafe and Whirlwind; slows from other players (PvP); a map’s attack speed for mercenaries.

    Damage Calculator¶

    Season 14 · Physical and elemental damage · Attack speed

    Compare up to four attack builds against any boss or map monster: damage per second, kill time, attack speed breakpoints, and what each stat is worth.

    The monster

    What a map’s monsters do to you, their hits against your life and resistances: the Monster Damage Calculator.

    Builds

    Pick an example in Start from… on a card, then put in your own numbers. Under each part of a build, what it makes; at the bottom of the screen, every build’s damage per second. Build A, the first card, is the one the others are compared with. To weigh weapons for one character, switch to Compare weapons; boots, belts, amulets, and the rest of your gear, to Compare gear. To fill in a weapon you have, hover over it in game, press Ctrl+C, and paste it into Paste a weapon from the game at the top of its Weapon section.

    Your character, skill, gear, and buffs go in the first card; every weapon card uses them and has only the weapon’s own fields, with what it does under them. Weapon A is the one the others are compared with. To fill in a weapon you have, hover over it in game, press Ctrl+C, and paste it into Paste a weapon from the game at the top of its card.

    Your build goes in the card, wearing what you wear now. Pick a slot, then in Now the item you wear there (it comes off for the other columns), and up to three items to try instead: each column is your build wearing that item, with the rest the same. Pick an item from the list (its best rolls to start, each roll a box), paste one copied in game (hover over it and press Ctrl+C), or type the stats of a rare. A character loaded from the PD2 armory fills in Now.

    Fields marked ● gold differ from build A (on builds of A’s class).

    How they compare

    Side by side

    Every number behind each build’s damage per second. The best of a row is marked.

    Average hit: the damage a hit does on average, with critical and deadly strikes counted at their chance. Damage per second counts every hit an attack makes; crushing blow is in the kill time only, since it takes a share of the monster’s life left.

    Attack speed breakpoints

    The increased attack speed each build needs on its other gear for each frame count of its attack (its weapon’s IAS as set; its skills’ attack speed and its weapon’s speed counted), and its damage per second there.

    Stat trade-offs

    +%

    Flat damage counts the same number added to minimum and to maximum damage. A stat a build can’t gain this way shows a dash (critical and deadly strike stop at their caps).

    Damage profile

    Every hit’s damage against the monster, sorted (the hits that land: a miss does none): the left end is the weakest hit, the right end the strongest. Deadly and critical strikes make the steps on the right.

      The fight

      Each build fights the monster at the game’s speed, hit by hit, with the same rolls its kill time is worked out from: one of the many fights that kill time averages. Fight again plays another one, and Replay plays this one again.

      How it’s worked out

      • The weapon: an ethereal weapon has 5/4 of its base damage, rounded down. Enhanced damage on the weapon (its own, a superior base’s, its sockets’) raises only the base damage: base + base × enhanced damage / 100, rounded down; then its flat minimum and maximum damage are added.
      • Kicks (Dragon Talon) don’t use the weapon’s damage, its enhanced damage, or its elemental damage. A kick’s minimum is the boots’ kick damage + twice your “+X kick damage” (the game adds it twice with boots on) + flat minimum damage on other gear + the weapon’s “+X Damage” + Dexterity / 4; its maximum the same with the maxima and Dexterity / 3. Both are multiplied by (100 + Dragon Talon’s kick damage + Strength × the boots’ Strength bonus / 100 (every boots has 100: +1% a point) + your auras’ and buffs’ damage + enhanced damage on other gear + damage against demons or undead) / 100. A kick has no weapon mastery; its critical strike, deadly strike, crushing blow, and attack rating are other gear’s and the weapon’s. That is why a weapon with a big “+X Damage” (Astreon’s Iron Ward +100–125, The Redeemer +60–120, Black +20–30) does so much for a kick.
      • A weapon’s own stats (deadly strike and its multiplier, critical strike and its multiplier, crushing blow, attack rating and attack rating %, −% target defense, Ignore Target’s Defense, attack rating and damage against demons or undead) count on that weapon’s hits only, as its enhanced damage and IAS do; the same stats on other gear count on every hit. With two weapons only one weapon’s stats are on the character at a time. The hitting weapon’s are on while a hit’s damage, critical strike, deadly strike, and crushing blow are worked out, so an attack that hits with the first weapon only (a normal attack, Zeal) gets nothing from the second weapon’s. The first weapon is the one you equipped first. Whether a hit lands is rolled just before the game switches to the hitting weapon, with the stats that are on at that moment, so the attack rating stats (attack rating, attack rating %, −% target defense, Ignore Target’s Defense) come from another weapon at times: every attack starts with the right-hand weapon’s on (the weapon slot on the left of your inventory), so its first hit rolls with those, and each later hit with the weapon of the hit before it. With the first weapon in the right hand, Frenzy, Double Swing, Berserk, and the claw finishers roll both hits with the first weapon’s attack rating stats; with it in the left hand, a normal attack rolls with the second weapon’s. Whirlwind and Blade Dance roll each hit with the other weapon’s. The weapon mastery’s attack rating goes by the hitting weapon. A thrown weapon or an arrow gets its damage, critical strike, and deadly strike when it is thrown, from the weapon that throws it; it rolls to hit where it lands, so with two throwing weapons that depends on how long it flies: each counts its own here. Your character screen’s attack rating shows the stats that are on: the first weapon’s after you equip, the right-hand weapon’s once you attack.
      • The attack: the weapon’s damage plus flat damage from other gear, times (100 + the attack skill’s damage + your auras’ and buffs’ damage + Strength × the weapon’s Strength bonus / 100 + Dexterity × its Dexterity bonus / 100 + your weapon mastery + damage against demons or undead when the monster is one + enhanced damage from other gear) / 100; a melee hit with a blunt weapon (a mace, hammer, club, scepter, wand, or staff) adds 50 more against undead, an arrow or a thrown weapon doesn’t. Your character screen shows it in whole points, without the part against demons or undead and with your elemental damage added (below); there the weapon’s “+X Damage” and Bash’s added damage come after the percentages. A minimum below 1 becomes 1 and a maximum at or below the minimum becomes the minimum + 1, before the percentages. The game keeps it in 256ths of a point; a hit is any damage from the minimum to the maximum alike. A skill that deals part of the weapon’s damage (Stun, Guided Arrow, Blade Fury) takes that part. Blade Fury’s and Blade Sentinel’s blades add the skill’s own damage (raised by their synergies) to it, and the percentages raise both, Claw and Dagger Mastery’s too. Bash’s added damage comes after the percentages, not raised by them. Concentrate turns 30% + 2% for each point put in it of the hit’s physical damage, after crit and deadly strike, into magic damage, which the monster’s magic resistance lowers instead of its physical resistance; crushing blow stays physical.
      • Your own aura gives you its full damage bonus; a party member’s or your mercenary’s gives half (the “Party Damage” of the tooltip).
      • Elemental damage: each of fire, lightning, cold, and magic is one roll per hit over everything you have of it: the weapon’s own (on its hits only, as its enhanced damage, with what is socketed in it), other gear’s and charms’, and the Holy auras, a Paladin’s own Holy Fire, Holy Freeze, Holy Shock, or Sanctuary and those items give any class (Dream’s Holy Shock, Hand of Justice’s Holy Fire, Doom’s Holy Freeze…): their damage on attacks, each at its level (two items with one aura add their levels; your own and an item’s count once, the higher), with your own points in its synergies. The element’s +% skill damage (“+12% to Fire Skill Damage” on Rainbow Facets, charms, and other gear) raises its minimum and maximum on your attacks too. A skill that deals part of the weapon’s damage deals that part of the elemental damage too. Poison is dealt over its length, a little every frame, and doesn’t stack: a new poison of yours replaces the one running only when it deals at least as much a frame, and then runs its own length from that moment; a weaker one is lost. So several poisons at once count their strongest roll, and hitting faster than the length can leave gaps, when a weaker roll is dropped while the stronger one runs out (several poison items: their lengths are averaged; Venom’s length replaces theirs). Your −% to enemy poison resistance also makes it last longer, by half as much on a monster (−20%: 10% longer); Diablo Clone, Rathma, and Lucion shorten poison (by 50%, 30%, and 30%), less your −%. Enhanced damage, the attack skill’s damage, Strength, Dexterity, and your weapon mastery never raise elemental damage, and critical and deadly strikes double only the physical part. Your character screen adds your elemental damage to the attack’s, each element in whole points: fire, lightning, and cold with their skill damage, magic, and poison over its length. From 10,000 on it shows thousands, rounded (12K).
      • Elemental resistances: the monster’s own, minus Conviction (fire, cold, lightning), Lower Resist (fire, lightning, cold, poison), and Sanctuary (magic: a Paladin’s own or an item’s), which count half against a monster whose own resistance is 100% or more (immune): a fire-immune monster at 120% under level 12 Conviction (−34%) is at 103%, still immune. Your gear’s −% enemy resistance lowers what is left only below 100%, and whatever is then below 0% counts half, at most −100%, as for physical damage. A map’s “Monsters absorb X%” (at most 40%) takes that share of what gets through off and heals the monster with it.
      • Critical strike comes first: chance (Amazon’s Critical Strike, items, a mastery) at most 75%, double damage plus the critical strike multiplier. Only a hit that isn’t critical can be a deadly strike: chance at most 75% plus maximum deadly strike, 1.5 times damage plus the deadly strike multiplier.
      • Physical resistance: the monster’s resistance plus its curses, Amplify Damage and Decrepify (Weaken, Attract, and Lower Resist leave it), and Battle Cry, which is no curse and goes with any. A monster holds as many curses as the most that any one cursing it can apply: one, one more at 10 and at 20 base points of Curse Mastery, and one more for each “you may apply an additional curse” (Raekor’s Virtue, Darkforce Spawn, Ebonbane, Gloom, Last Wish); an Act 4 mercenary applies two. What a build puts on it itself counts first (Curses and debuffs on its card: its own skill, charges, a chance to cast on striking), then your mercenary’s, then the party’s (Curses from others, the monster’s fields); the same curse twice counts once, the stronger. Of a build’s own Battle Cry (Call to Arms’ too) and the others’, the higher counts. Against a monster whose own resistance is 100% or more, curses and Battle Cry count half. Physical pierce lowers what is left below 100%. Whatever is then below 0% counts half against your hits, with pierce or without: Amplify Damage’s −34% on a monster with no resistance is −17% (at most −100%). Damage is lowered by the resistance (raised at negative resistance, at most double).
      • Chance to hit: every hit rolls on its own, and only a hit that lands does damage or a crushing blow. Attack rating = (5 × Dexterity − 35 + the class’s own (Amazon and Druid 5, Assassin 15, Barbarian and Paladin 20, Necromancer −10, Sorceress −15) + attack rating on gear and from Grim Ward, + attack rating against demons or undead when the monster is one) × (100 + attack rating % on gear + from your auras and buffs (Fanaticism, Concentration, Blessed Aim, Heart of Wolverine, a Holy aura (yours or an item’s), a wereform’s by Lycanthropy’s level, Penetrate’s level) + the attack skill’s own + your weapon mastery’s) / 100, rounded down. The mastery: on a thrown hit with a throwing weapon, Throwing Mastery’s; on a melee hit, One Handed, Two Handed, or Claw and Dagger Mastery’s by the weapon; on an arrow or bolt, none. Your character screen shows it with the attack on a mouse button, without the part against demons or undead. The monster’s defense (a boss’s in Hell at its own level) is first lowered in points by Inner Sight and Attract, then by Conviction, Battle Cry (at most 30%), Weaken, and a Fanatic champion’s own −70%, added up; below 0 it adds to your attack rating. Ignore Target’s Defense makes it 0 against a normal monster, a champion, or a minion, not against a unique, superunique, boss, or player; −% target defense (your gear’s, and your own Blessed Aim’s, its base points) lowers it by that share, half against a superunique, a boss, or a player, at most 100%. Chance to hit = 2 × 111 × attack rating / (attack rating + defense) × your level / (your level + its level), at least 5%; above 95% what is left of the 111 counts 90/111, at most 100%, every step rounded down. Smite and Guided Arrow never miss; Bash, Concentrate, Feral Rage, and Maul roll as the swing starts, and their hit lands only if that roll did.
      • Crushing blow takes a share of the life the monster has left, whatever its life: 1/8 for a monster (champions and uniques too, and Andariel, Duriel, and the quest bosses such as Izual and Nihlathak), 1/30 for a map boss, 1/10 for a player or a pet, and 1/70 for another boss (Mephisto, Diablo, Baal, the ubers, Diablo Clone, Rathma, Lucion, The Tainted Hive, Na-Krul, the two Djinns, Zoltun Kulle) at full life, less as it loses life (1/(70 + 10 for every 1% it is missing)). Each player after the first adds 1.4 to the 8, 30, or 70 (their 70% more life, / 50). A ranged attack divides by 1.5 times as much; crushing blow efficiency multiplies the share by (1 + efficiency / 100). The monster’s physical resistance lowers it (none at 100% or more; pierce doesn’t count). More chance and more efficiency are always better; the kill time shows how much, beside the time without it.
      • Frames per attack come from the IAS Calculator, which has the whole model and plays each attack frame by frame: speed = 100 + the skills’ speed (Fanaticism, Frenzy by its charges, Burst of Speed, Werewolf, the attack’s own) − the weapon’s speed modifier + effective IAS (120 × IAS / (120 + IAS)), 30 less for a sequence, kept between 15 and 175; an animation of N frames takes N × 256 / (its speed × speed / 100) frames, rounded up, minus 1, held. A normal attack is attack 1 or attack 2, half the time each, so it counts both, and it hits with the first weapon only. Two weapons: only the faster one’s speed counts. Zeal, Fend, Fury, Strafe, and Dragon Talon rewind their animation between hits (Strafe: its fewest arrows, at one monster); Whirlwind and Blade Dance hit every 6 frames, with each weapon, whatever the attack speed, slows, or Frenzy’s charges; Charge’s strike runs at a speed the game sets when it connects, and its damage per second and kill time count the strikes back to back (the run before each strike isn’t counted). A druid’s wereform attacks run at a speed set by the weapon’s attack speed in human form.
      • Next breakpoint: the increased attack speed a build still needs on its other gear (its weapon’s IAS as set) for fewer frames, and what that is worth in damage per second. A build at the most its attack counts shows none.
      • Map monsters are the map’s own, its bosses and minibosses too, at the map’s level (a boss at its own), with their resistances in Hell. Kind makes one of its normal monsters a champion (twice its life and 2 levels higher; Possessed 4 times its life; Berserker half its life and 3 levels higher; Ghostly at 80% physical resistance; Fanatic at −70% defense, added to the curses’), a unique’s minion (1.5 times its life, 3 levels higher), or a unique (twice its life, 3 levels higher). The level counts in the chance to hit; the defense stays the normal monster’s. A unique or superunique made of it gets the mods you tick, in the order ticked, as the game applies them in the order it rolled them: Magic Resistant +40% cold, then fire, then lightning (each below 100%); Fire, Lightning, or Cold Enchanted +75% to that element, even on an immune one; Mana Burn +20% magic; Spectral Hit +20% cold, then fire, then lightning (each below 75%); Stone Skin +50% physical and twice the defense. A monster that already has two immunities gets none of these resistances (Stone Skin still doubles its defense). In Hell a unique has 3 random mods of about 12, each about 1 in 4; a superunique its own, then 2 random ones. Nothing caps them: Fire Enchanted on a 120% one makes 195%. A unique’s minions take its mods’ damage, not their resistances, and champions none (Ghostly sets physical resistance to 80%). A map starts on its toughest monster for build A: of the monsters found all through it, the one whose life takes the most of build A’s hits, its defense and resistances counted.
      • The bosses have their life, defense, and resistances in Hell: the life of the boss’s own level, +70% for each player after the first (at most 8,388,607). Diablo Clone, Rathma, Mendeln, and Lucion have 1.5 times that at tier 0, 4.8 times at tier 1, and 5.6 times at tier 2; their phases, immunity, healing, and helpers aren’t counted. The map and dungeon bosses on the game’s map boss lists take crushing blow as map bosses (1/30 of their life); The Tainted Hive, Na-Krul, the two Djinns, and Zoltun Kulle aren’t on them and take it as other bosses do. Andariel and Duriel take it as monsters do (1/8 of their life left), but Ignore Target’s Defense and −% target defense treat them as bosses. Of a map’s modifiers, only absorb is counted (the monster’s Absorbs fields).
      • How they compare: damage per second is the average hit × what critical and deadly strikes add × what the monster’s resistance lets through × hits per second × the chance to hit, so a build’s difference from build A splits exactly into those five. With elemental damage on either build it splits into the hit on the monster (its physical and elemental parts named), the chance to hit, and hits per second. When a build differs from build A only in its weapon (every build in Compare weapons), it says so first: what the other weapon does to damage per hit, attack speed and the next breakpoint, critical and deadly strikes, crushing blow and the kill time, and the chance to hit. On a build of build A’s class, the fields that differ from build A are marked gold (across classes nearly every field would be).
      • The Fight: each lane plays one of the kill time’s own fights, hit by hit, at the game’s speed: the same rolls, misses, critical and deadly strikes, and crushing blows. A spell’s, trap’s, or sentinel’s damage counts there as it lands, so its fights take longer than the kill time, which counts each cast in full when it’s cast; under each kill, the average of the lane’s own fights stands beside the kill time.
      • Kills within 1 to 6 hits: 4,000 fights of up to six hits against the monster, with damage rolls, critical and deadly strikes, crushing blow, misses, and elemental damage as in the kill time: the share of them that end within each number of hits. Shown when a fight takes six hits or fewer on average; Stat Trade-offs then lists what one more item’s worth of each stat does to them.
      • Compare gear: each column is build A with the item it wears in that slot now (Now) taken off and the column’s item put on. The item’s enhanced damage, flat damage, attack speed, critical and deadly strike, crushing blow, attack rating, elemental damage, Strength, and Dexterity change Other gear and the character’s numbers; its + skills raise the levels of the attack, the masteries, and your own auras and buffs; boots bring their kick damage. An item from the list starts at its best rolls. Set bonuses aren’t counted again when a set item comes or goes, and an item’s auras, chance to cast, and poison aren’t counted.
      • Skills that read another skill’s level: Dragon Claw’s damage counts the level of Claw and Dagger Mastery as its tooltip does; Penetrate’s base points are both Strafe’s synergy and its pierce.
      • Battle Command counts as cast twice, as players cast it: its level with your + all skills (a Call to Arms’ too), then its own + all skills on top. It adds 20% damage and 3% more a level, and +1 to every skill you have while it lasts (+1 more for every 10 base points a Barbarian puts in it): your attack’s level, your masteries, and your own auras and buffs. Burst of Speed and Fade can’t both be on: a character loaded from the PD2 armory keeps the one with more points (Burst of Speed on a tie), and Fade instead switches Burst of Speed off.
      • Fists of Fire: every hit that lands casts the next charge’s effect, 1, 2, 3, then 1 again (the fourth hit clears the three charges first); a miss casts nothing and gains no charge. Each charge held adds +50% attack rating to the next hit. The hit itself is your weapon’s, as a normal attack’s. Charge 1 turns the hit’s physical damage into fire (3% for each skill level, all of it from level 34; the turned part gets no fire skill damage) and adds the skill’s own fire, then an explosion of that fire around the monster and ground flames that burn whatever stands in them every frame for 2.6 seconds: about 1 under a small monster, 3 under a normal-size one, and 5 under a big one (its Size). Charge 2: a fire nova centered on the monster, which hits it once (a small monster not at all). Charge 3: meteors of fire and physical damage (no +% damage reaches them, Battle Command’s neither), 2 on a small or normal-size monster and 3 on a big one, with the flames of the one landing on it for 1 second (3 under a normal-size monster, 5 under a big one). Crushing blow still works on every hit. Damage per second averages the three charges; the meteors land about a second after the hit and the flames burn over time, which the kill time counts at the hit. Your character screen shows the charge your next hit casts: on charge 1 the attack’s damage there plus the skill’s fire and one ground flame for 2 seconds, on charge 2 plus the nova, on charge 3 plus one meteor’s fire; the flames, the nova, and the meteor without your fire skill damage. With charges held, its Attack line (a normal attack is a finishing move) adds the latest charge’s damage as above and +50% attack rating a charge.
      • Fire Claws, as a werewolf or a werebear: as the swing starts you gain the skill’s fire damage (Molten Boulder’s and Armageddon’s points raise it), which the hit deals with your gear’s fire, both raised by +% fire skill damage. Each hit that lands sends 4 firestorms, +1 for each 5 points put in the skill, toward the monster; they leave flames behind, and a monster standing in them takes a flame’s fire about 5 times (once every 5 frames while the flames last), as the tooltip’s Firestorm Damage counts them: each flame deals about half the skill’s fire.
      • Vengeance hits as a normal attack does, then each hit that lands sets off an explosion on the monster: fire, cold, and lightning, each the flat damage of that element on your gear plus Vengeance’s own (its synergies in), all raised by its Elemental Damage % and that element’s skill damage. A Holy aura’s damage and Enchant’s aren’t in the explosion, only in the hit. The explosion also hits the monsters around it and chains on, which isn’t counted.
      • Rabies is the werewolf’s bite with the skill’s poison: its damage over 5 seconds, raised by Poison Creeper’s, Feral Rage’s, and Lycanthropy’s points and by +% poison skill damage. Poison on your gear adds its damage each second; one such item adds its length to the 5 seconds, and with several the game adds all their lengths to the 5 seconds and divides by how many there are. The poison doesn’t stack, and it spreads to the monsters nearby, which isn’t counted.
      • Poison Strike is a Necromancer’s dagger or scythe hit with the skill’s poison over 2 seconds, raised by Desecrate’s and Bone Armor’s points and by +% poison skill damage, your gear’s poison added as with Rabies. While the attack plays, it pierces 5% + 1% a level (at most 50%) of the monster’s poison resistance, on that poison only; like −% enemy poison resistance it also makes the poison last longer. Its clouds poison the monsters around, which isn’t counted.
      • Claws of Thunder and Phoenix Strike charge up the same way too. Claws of Thunder: charge 1 adds the skill’s lightning to the hit; charge 2 bursts a nova out of the monster that hits it once; charge 3 bursts charged bolts out of it, one of which hits it, and that bolt leaves a storm that hits it once more. Dragon Claw’s points raise all of it. Phoenix Strike adds nothing to the hit itself: charge 1 drops a meteor on the monster’s spot about half a second later, and its flames burn under the monster for a second (none under a small one, about 3 under a normal-size one, 5 under a big one); charge 2 sends chain lightning curving out of the monster, which hits it once and usually a second time when one loops back about a second later; charge 3 bursts chaos ice out of it, one of which hits it. Fists of Fire’s points raise its fire, Claws of Thunder’s its lightning, and Blades of Ice’s its cold. Each charge held adds +25% attack rating with Phoenix Strike, +50% with Claws of Thunder.
      • Blades of Ice charges up the same way: every hit that lands casts the next charge, 1, 2, 3, then 1 again, and each charge held adds +50% attack rating to the next hit. Charge 1 adds the skill’s cold to the hit. Charge 2 sends a wave toward the monster that leaves trails to both sides; the monster takes the wave’s hit and one trail’s. Charge 3 bursts a ring of 11 ice bolts out of the monster; one of them hits it, the others fly on to the monsters around it. Tiger Strike’s points and +% cold skill damage raise all of them; the chill isn’t counted. Your character screen shows the charge your next hit casts: the attack’s damage there plus the skill’s cold, a wave, or an ice bolt, those two without your cold skill damage.
      • The elemental arrows carry the skill’s own element (its synergies and that element’s skill damage in) with your weapon’s damage and your gear’s elemental damage, and an arrow deals a share of all of it: Magic Arrow 3/4, Fire Arrow and Cold Arrow half, the others all. Magic, Fire, and Cold Arrow then turn half of the arrow’s physical damage into their element, after critical and deadly strikes. Their other arrows reach the monster while it can’t be hit again, so it takes one arrow a shot. A Fire Arrow that hits leaves a flame under the monster that burns it for 2 seconds. Exploding Arrow: an arrow that hits explodes with the skill’s fire and your gear’s (an explosion deals only its own element), then drops 3 bombs, +1 for each 5 levels (at most 8), behind the monster, each an explosion at the points you put in the skill (no + skills); about 0.46 of 3 bombs reach the monster, 1.3 of 4 or 5, 2.25 of 6 or 7, and 3.1 of 8. Immolation Arrow explodes hit or miss, with the skill’s fire and your gear’s, and leaves a disc of flames that burn for 2 seconds without fire skill damage: 1 under a small monster, 5 under a normal-size one, and 9 under a big one. Freezing Arrow’s arrow is your weapon’s and your gear’s damage alone; its explosion, hit or miss, is the skill’s cold and your gear’s. A part that comes on a miss too counts whole in the damage per second, the rest only when the arrow hits.
      • Lightning Fury’s javelin never misses: your weapon’s damage with the skill’s lightning and your gear’s elemental damage, then a bolt of the skill’s lightning (no weapon or gear damage) to each monster near it, the one it hit among them: one bolt on a monster alone, more in a pack (not counted). Charged Strike, Lightning Strike, and Power Strike add the skill’s lightning to the hit, then, hit or miss: Charged Strike’s bolts start on the monster, and all of them hit a big monster, about half a normal-size one (the ones that leave straight rather than diagonally), and none a small one, and their number is also added to the hit’s damage % (+3% to +12%); Lightning Strike’s chain lightning hits the monster once before it jumps on to others (not counted); Power Strike’s nova hits it once with one of its bolts (a small monster not at all). Their synergies and +% lightning skill damage raise the lightning; Power Strike’s own lightning keeps a minimum of 1.
      • Split Throw throws 3 weapons, one more for every 5 levels up to 7, fanned out a step apart. The first to reach the monster is the throw’s hit; the others pass through it, fly to the end of their range and come back over it about 2 seconds later, each its own hit: your weapon’s damage without Split Throw’s own %, with your critical and deadly strike and your gear’s elemental damage, rolling to hit as the throw does, whether the throw hit or not. From 5 yards, 3 of them cross a small monster, 5 a normal-size one, and all of them a big one, so a throw at level 20 brings 4 returns on a normal-size monster. Each return can crush and poison as the throw does, and when the throw’s own weapon pierces the monster (your pierce chance: gear’s “Pierce” and Throwing Mastery’s) it comes back too.
      • The javelins: Poison Javelin puts the skill’s poison on the javelin with your gear’s (1 second, plus your poison items’ lengths as for any hit); the clouds it leaves behind lie where the javelin has already passed, so they never reach a monster standing alone. Plague Javelin does the same with its 3-second poison, then, hit or miss, bursts a ring of 23 clouds out of the monster (about 17 reach a small one), each the skill’s poison alone (not your gear’s); with poisons replacing each other only when at least as strong, the monster ends up with nearly the best roll. Its 1-second cooldown is in its frames. Lightning Bolt never misses: all of the javelin’s physical damage turns into lightning after critical and deadly strikes, added to the skill’s lightning and your gear’s; your gear’s fire, cold, magic, and poison don’t come with it. A javelin that pierces flies on and its clouds can reach the monster after all: not counted.
      • Enchant and Cold Enchant count on every build: once cast, they work like “Adds X–Y fire damage” (or cold) on your gear, on every attack (melee hits, kicks, arrows, thrown weapons, both weapons), rolled with your gear’s, raised by +% fire (or cold) skill damage. Missiles get all of it, not a third; an arrow that deals part of your elemental damage (Magic Arrow 3/4, Fire Arrow half) deals the same part of them. Explosions carry them with your gear’s fire or cold; a skill’s own bolts and flames don’t. The Sorceress who casts it gets all of it; other players and mercenaries get half (each number halved, rounded down). Both can be on at once, and each adds its attack rating bonus. The skill’s table damage counts half: Enchant 1 adds 12–17 fire, not 25–35.
      • A form from an item: any class can fight as a werewolf or a werebear when an item gives the skill (Beast: Werebear and Maul; Wolfhowl: Werewolf), at its level with your + all skills. The form’s speed comes from your class’s own attack with the weapon, as the IAS Calculator shows; Werebear adds its damage and attack rating bonus, Lycanthropy is a Druid’s only. The form stays when you put the item away: cast it with Beast on your weapon swap, swap back, and the form takes its speed from the weapon in hand. That weapon’s own IAS counts the most there: with 120% IAS on it (6 Shael), a Sorceress’s werebear attacks in 4 frames with some bases (daggers, Champion Axe, Giant Thresher, Walking Stick) and 5 with others (Phase Blade, Flail, Elder Staff); IAS on other gear barely changes it. A character loaded from the PD2 armory starts in that form when it has no attack skill of its own here, an item on either weapon set giving it.
      • Smite hits with your shield’s damage (an ethereal one’s isn’t raised), plus Holy Shield’s while it is up (the level it was cast at, +5% for each point in Smite and Defiance) and every item’s “+X Damage”, never your weapon’s damage. Its damage % (+75%, +25% a level), your auras’, all of your Strength (a shield’s Strength bonus is 100), and other gear’s enhanced damage raise it, the shield’s included; your weapon’s own enhanced damage doesn’t. Then 0 up to 15–41 more physical damage by its level, raised by nothing. It never misses and has no critical or deadly strike, elemental or poison damage, or damage against demons or undead. Crushing blow: 5% + 1% for each point put in it, with your gear’s, at its efficiency. Its stun isn’t counted. Your character screen’s Smite line adds your enhanced damage instead of multiplying by it, so it shows less than a hit does.
      • Dragon Tail kicks as Dragon Talon does, without a damage %, then a kick that lands sets off an explosion around the monster (4 yards): a second kick’s damage with its own critical and deadly strike, all of it fire, raised by its Fire Damage % (Dragon Talon’s and Dragon Flight’s points add to it) and your fire skill damage. The explosion carries nothing else. Charges you hold aren’t counted (it releases them, as a finishing move).
      • Blade Sentinel is thrown at attack speed. Each cast sends a sentinel flying from you through the monster you cast it on and back for about 2 seconds. Its blade hits what it passes with all of your weapon’s damage and the skill’s own (raised by Blade Fury’s and Blade Shield’s points), raised by your enhanced damage, Claw and Dagger Mastery, Strength, Dexterity, and auras, with your gear’s elemental damage. All your sentinels together hit a monster with their blades at most once every 6 frames, so casting faster soon stops adding blade hits. A blade rolls to hit with the sentinel’s attack rating: yours before its percentages, +5 for each level of the skill, and the skill’s own attack bonus, then your attack rating %, the skill’s bonus, and Claw and Dagger Mastery’s; it pierces half your physical pierce and none of your −% enemy resistance. Each time a sentinel turns round, at most once every half second, it bursts on everything within 4.7 yards for 75% of a melee hit’s damage (75% of the skill’s own, not raised), with your own attack rating (your attack rating % counts twice), your physical pierce, and your −% enemy resistance. The page counts the blade hits and bursts that reach a monster standing alone, cast on it from the Skill section’s Cast from; the poison of your gear and Venom comes with every blade, and each sentinel keeps its own on the monster, so several run at once (about 3 with Venom when you keep casting); the bursts carry 75% of it as your own poison. Crushing blow isn’t counted for it.
      • Dragon Flight teleports you to the monster and kicks it once, then waits for its cooldown (2 seconds at level 1, 1 second from level 26), however fast you attack. Its kick isn’t Dragon Talon’s: it takes your boots’ kick damage (“+X kick damage” twice) raised by its Kick Damage % (+100%, +25% a level), Strength × your boots’ Strength bonus, the auras’ and buffs’ damage, and enhanced damage on gear that isn’t a weapon, plus your gear’s elemental damage. Your Dexterity, flat damage, “+X damage”, damage against demons or undead, critical strike, and deadly strike don’t count, though your character screen adds some of them. As a finisher it works as Dragon Tail does (one kick, the charges go off once when it lands, a miss keeps them), and a cycle lasts at least its cooldown.
      • Dragon Talon as a finisher: its first kick rolls with the charges’ attack rating and, landing, gets Tiger Strike’s damage or charge 1’s element and sets the three charges off once; its other kicks then have none of them. If the first kick misses, the other kicks still get the charges but can’t set them off, so the next Dragon Talon tries again.
      • Dragon Tail as a finisher works the same way with one kick: its attack rating gets +50% for each charge held (Phoenix Strike’s +25%), and a kick that lands gets Tiger Strike’s damage or charge 1’s element, sets the three charges off once, and uses them up; its explosion (a second kick’s damage as fire) gets none of the charges. A kick that misses keeps the charges, and you kick again.
      • Dragon Claw as a finisher: set its level under Skill on a charge-up skill (Tiger Strike, Fists of Fire, Claws of Thunder, Blades of Ice, Phoenix Strike), and the numbers count a cycle of three charges, then a Dragon Claw. Its two hits, your first claw then the other, get its own damage (+50% and 25% a level, +20% for each point in Weapon Block and each level of Claw Mastery), +50% attack rating for each charge held (Phoenix Strike’s +25%), Tiger Strike’s damage for three charges, and charge 1’s element of Fists of Fire, Claws of Thunder or Blades of Ice. After each of the two hits, landed or not, the three charges go off again (the nova, the bolts, the meteors…), then they are used up. The second hit comes a few frames after the first, and each kind of missile hits a monster once in a few frames, so at a high attack speed the second hit’s novas and bolts miss; explosions, meteors, and flames always count. Damage per second is the cycle’s damage over its time, misses in. A charge-up skill’s own hits are always your first claw’s: the game takes the first weapon for an attack’s first hit and the other for its second.
      • Tiger Strike charges up as Fists of Fire does (+50% attack rating for each charge held), and its charges are damage: the first hit of a cycle gets +50% damage + 25% a level, the second twice that, the third three times that, added to your other damage bonuses on the weapon’s damage; the fourth hit starts again. Charges 2 and 3 also widen an item’s melee splash.
      • Cobra Strike charges up as Fists of Fire does (+50% attack rating for each charge held). Charge 1 fires 7 poison bolts in a fan at the monster and charge 3 sends 64 poison clouds out of it; each carries its own poison (raised 20% by each point in Venom and by your poison skill damage) plus your hit’s poison from gear and Venom, and a monster standing alone takes one of each. Charge 2 adds 60% + 30% a level to the hit’s damage and steals life and mana. Your poisons don’t add up: the strongest one runs, from your hits, bolts, and clouds alike.
      • Blade Shield, while it is on, strikes every monster within 2.7 yards of you every 20 frames, a frame sooner for each 2 points put in it (10 frames at 20), whatever your attack speed, and rolls to hit with your attack rating and the skill’s bonus. Its tooltip says 25% of your weapon’s damage, but the game works the damage out twice (25%, then 25% of that plus another 25%): 31% of your weapon’s damage raised by your percentages, with critical and deadly strikes rolled each time, 31% of your gear’s elemental damage, and poison twice as long. The tooltip’s Damage line (the skill’s own damage, raised by Blade Sentinel’s and Blade Fury’s points) isn’t dealt. The page counts it as an attack of its own, on a monster next to you; beside another attack it adds to that one (not counted together).
      • Spells deal their own damage, raised by their synergies and your skill damage of their element, through the monster’s resistance of that element; your weapon’s damage, enhanced damage, the auras’ and buffs’ damage, critical and deadly strike, and crushing blow don’t count, and they never miss. Their speed comes from faster cast rate. Fist of the Heavens strikes the monster with its lightning, then sends a holy bolt of magic at each monster within 13.3 yards (up to 50): the monster you cast it on takes one. Holy Bolt fires 1 bolt, 2 from level 15 and 3 from level 25, fanning out from you and passing one step to each side of the monster you cast it on: every one hits a normal-size or big monster standing alone, and a small one from close up; from 5 yards or more a side bolt passes a small one about 1 time in 5 (its Cast from field). Both hit every monster, not only undead or demons. Holy Nova bursts out from you and hits each monster within 18 yards once, with a 4-second cooldown; undead and demons take it whole whatever their magic resistance. Holy Light hits the monster you cast it on and every monster within 4 yards of it. A Sorceress’s Lightning, Chain Lightning, and Nova each hit a monster once a cast with the skill’s lightning. So do her Fire Bolt and Ice Bolt, which burst on what they hit (2 yards), Fire Ball (it explodes, 3.3 yards), Glacial Spike (it bursts, 4 yards), and Ice Blast (it flies on through). Frost Nova hits a monster standing alone once. Combustion sends out a ring of 32 fire balls, each exploding on the first monster it meets; they reach a lone monster together, and once one has hit it the others can’t for the next 2 frames, so a normal-size monster takes 1 and a big one next to you about 1.7 (a small one farther off can stand between two balls). Frozen Orb lets go a bolt every frame of its flight, 45 in all, and ends in a ring of 16; the orb itself deals nothing, and it always flies 14 yards, wherever you click. A monster the orb flies through takes about 8 of them if it is normal-size, 2 to 3 if it is small, and 10 if it is big, most of them while the orb is on it; cast from 14 yards, where the orb’s flight ends on the monster and its ring starts there, about 13 a normal-size one and 18 a big one. Blizzard drops 25 shards over 2 seconds anywhere within 4.7 yards of the spot, and each bursts on every monster within 2.7 yards of where it lands: about 6 reach a monster standing on the spot. Ice Barrage fires 3 lances, 4 from 3 points put in it and 5 from 6, fanning out the same way, each bursting where it hits: from close up about 3 of them hit a normal-size monster standing alone (with 5, a big one about 4), from 5 yards or more about 2.5 (a big one 3); a small one takes 1 (Cast from again). Both are counted cast on the monster: clicking the ground beyond it narrows the fan where it stands, so more of them hit (5 lances on a normal-size monster 5 yards away: about 4.5 instead of 2.5 when you click twice as far), and clicking short of it spreads them wider, so fewer do. Charged Bolt’s bolts wander out from you and spread: about half of them hit a normal-size monster you stand next to, a third from 5 yards, and a fifth from 10 (its Cast from field). Meteor lands 0.6 seconds after the cast with physical and fire damage in equal parts, both raised by its synergies and your fire skill damage; then its flames burn for 1.6 seconds, about 3 under a normal-size monster, 5 under a big one, and none under a small one. Cooldowns are in the damage per second: Meteor 0.5 seconds, Blizzard 0.9, Combustion 2.5. Inferno, held, breathes every 4 frames whatever your faster cast rate: 2 flames, 1 more every 4 levels up to 5, fanning out toward the monster; after one hits, the others can’t for 2 frames, so a normal-size monster takes about 1.4 a breath (a small one 1, a big one 1.8). Each lowers its fire resistance by 4% + 1% a level (half a level from 22) for that hit and a moment after. Thunder Storm is cast once and strikes on its own every 2 seconds, 0.05 seconds sooner for each point put in it (1 second at 20), while you cast other spells: the strike’s lightning on a monster near you and a ring of novas from it, one of which hits a normal-size or big monster. Its damage per second is its own; add it to your main spell’s. Hydra and Lesser Hydra put up 3 heads a cast for 5 seconds, 9 at most: a new cast every 42 frames (1.7 seconds) keeps 9 up, and casting faster only replaces the oldest. Each head shoots at a monster within 16.7 yards on 6 of 10 of its thinks, waiting 0.6 seconds after each shot and idling 0.4 seconds otherwise: about 3.2 shots in its life, every one on a monster standing alone. The heads have your fire skill damage but not your −% enemy fire resistance. Fire Wall cast on a monster burns it with about 2 flames if it is small, 4 if it is normal-size, and 5 if it is big, each every frame for 3 seconds; recast after its 1.5-second cooldown, two walls burn at once. Blaze cast next to a monster explodes around you, then leaves burning ground for 1.4 seconds: about 5 flames under a small monster, 12 under a normal-size one, and 17 under a big one, each every frame; a 2-second cooldown. The burning ground it leaves behind as you run isn’t counted. A Necromancer’s Teeth (3 teeth, 1 more a level, at most 24) and Bone Spear (1 spear, 2 from level 15, 3 from 25) fan out side by side, but once one has hit a monster the others can’t hit it for a moment, so a monster standing alone takes one a cast; the rest fly on to the monsters around it. Bone Spirit flies to its monster and hits it once. Corpse Explosion blows up a corpse: 5% to 10% of the corpse’s life (less when the corpse is above your level), half of it fire and half physical, plus the skill’s own physical and fire damage, on every monster near it; the Skill section asks for the corpse’s life (0: one like the monster). Dark Pact needs curses on the monster: its magic damage once with one curse, then 1.1 times more for each further curse (3.2 times with three), and it takes the monster’s other curses off, so each cycle casts your curses again before it. A loaded bone Necromancer who maxes Teeth, Bone Spear, and Bone Spirit starts as Teeth. A Druid’s Tornado wanders out from you, hits a monster once on its way (about 3 times in 4 when it stands next to you, less from farther) and bursts every 0.6 seconds on everything within 2.7 yards of it. Twister sends out many twisters, but once one has hit a monster the others can’t for 0.4 seconds: one a cast on a monster standing alone. Arctic Blast works like Inferno with 2 streams a breath, lowering cold resistance by 5% + 1% a level. Hurricane and Armageddon run on their own once cast: Hurricane hits every monster near you every 0.8 seconds; Armageddon drops a rock every 9 frames on a random spot near you, and about 3 in 10 of them reach a monster next to you. Their damage per second adds to your main skill’s. Molten Boulder rolls from you and hits a monster each time it rolls onto it: a monster it can’t knock back takes 2 to 6 hits as it rolls through, one it knocks back (half the time, a small one every time) is caught up with and hit again, and a large one breaks it at once; it explodes where it stops (a wall, a large monster, or the end of its roll) on everything within 4.7 yards and leaves burning ground on its way. Volcano rises on the monster’s spot for 6 seconds and hits what stands in it every 7 frames, one hit every 7 frames however many volcanoes stand there, while its debris rains small fires around it. Fissure opens a crack every frame (with 20 points put in it) for 2.2 seconds within 4 yards of the spot, never where another crack lies, each burning a monster it reaches once: about 3 on a normal-size monster standing there. Desecrate, cast on a corpse, raises 5 more corpses and a cloud that poisons the monster once for 3 seconds (counted with a corpse next to it). Poison Nova poisons the monster once a cast for 2 seconds, with your poison skill damage; poisons don’t stack: a new one replaces the one running only when it is at least as strong. An Assassin’s traps (Lightning Sentry, Chain Lightning Sentry, Charged Bolt Sentry, Wake of Fire, and Wake of Inferno) fire on their own: you can have 5 out, each fires a set number of times and then disappears, so laying a new one as each runs out keeps 5 firing (counted here), and attack speed only lays them faster. Each shot is the skill’s own damage with your skill damage of its element; your −% enemy resistance doesn’t reach them. Lightning Sentry’s bolt and Chain Lightning Sentry’s chain hit a monster standing alone once a shot; Charged Bolt Sentry’s bolts wander out, so only some reach it; Wake of Fire’s wave hits it once and its side flames now and then; Wake of Inferno’s 7 flames a burst each hit it. Death Sentry works like Corpse Explosion: its traps stay, and each explodes a corpse near the monster every 1.2 seconds (3–5% of the corpse’s life, half of it fire, with the trap’s own physical and fire), on everything within 14 yards of the corpse; the page counts a corpse for each explosion. Shock Web is thrown the same way, with a 1-second cooldown: 4 spikes, +1 every 3 levels, spreading wider with its level, each leaving a shock field for 3.6 seconds that burns a monster standing in it with lightning every frame; the page counts the fields that land under the monster, and the fields of your next throws add to them. An Assassin’s Fire Blast is thrown at attack speed: 1 bomb, +1 every 3 levels up to 9, each landing on a random spot around the monster (wider with more points put in it) and exploding for its fire on everything within 2.7 yards: about 3 in 4 reach the monster at 20 points. An Assassin’s Mind Blast hits the monster you cast it on once with its physical damage, raised by Psychic Hammer’s, Cloak of Shadows’, and Shadow Warrior’s points, through its physical resistance; the blast reaches every monster within 3.3 to 4.7 yards of it (by the points put in it), up to 4 yards more when you cast it again quickly. Her Psychic Hammer hits the monster you cast it on once with its magic damage, raised by Cloak of Shadows’, Mind Blast’s, and Shadow Warrior’s points and your magic skill damage, and never misses; the hammer then bounces to other monsters near it, each bounce dealing 20% of the first hit’s damage less than the one before (80%, 60%, …; a monster standing alone takes one hit). For Holy Bolt, Ice Barrage, Charged Bolt, Frozen Orb, Blizzard, Combustion, and Inferno, the Skill section’s Bolts that hit (lances, shards, fire balls) takes the number you see in game instead; 0 keeps the page’s. Her Fire, Lightning, and Cold Mastery count on all her spells and attacks: each adds its skill damage, and Fire and Cold Mastery lower the monster’s resistance.
      • Between cooldowns: a skill with a cooldown (Blizzard, Meteor, Combustion, Fire Wall, Blaze, Volcano, Fissure, Holy Nova, Shock Web, and Plague Javelin) leaves you standing from the end of its cast until the cooldown is over. Pick up to three other skills in the Skill section’s Between cooldowns lists to cast in that time: a spell between spells, a weapon attack between weapon attacks, each at its own speed. A channel such as Inferno goes breath by breath, a breath every 4 frames; a skill with a cooldown of its own, such as Fire Wall, goes whenever that cooldown allows, since every skill has its own. The page plays the fight out: your skill as soon as its cooldown allows, and in each wait the first of the others that is ready. It tries every order, casting either only what ends before your skill is ready again or until it is, and counts the rotation that does the most damage a second; when your skill alone does more, nothing else is counted. Inferno’s and Arctic Blast’s lowered fire or cold resistance lasts 10 frames after each breath: it counts on the other skills’ damage of that element that lands while it is on (Meteor’s when it lands, a burn such as Fire Wall’s for the frames it burns while it is on). Damage per second, the kill time, and the breakpoints then count the whole rotation.
      • Around it: a skill whose explosions, novas, bolts, clouds, or flames also reach other monsters says so under Skill, with the radius where it is one; only the chosen monster’s share counts in the numbers.
      • Not counted: the damage of skills that aren’t in the Attack list (Blessed Hammer, Leap Attack, Static Field, and summons other than Hydra and the Assassin’s traps), open wounds, life leech, mana, the monster’s blocking, a map’s modifiers other than absorb, splash damage to other monsters, and mercenaries.

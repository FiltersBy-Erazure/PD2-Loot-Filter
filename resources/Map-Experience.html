The “Experience” tag: how good the rolls are

A full clear of this map with its rolls, against the same map with no rolls: 1.48x means the rolls add 48%. Which map it is doesn't matter here, only what its rolls add to it, and your level doesn't change it.

  • What counts: Monster Density (more monsters), Monster Rarity (more champion and unique packs), Experience, Area Level (higher level monsters, each worth more), Fortified (half as many monsters, each worth double), and "Map contains ..." monsters (one more kind of monster in the mix).
  • What doesn't: anything that makes monsters easier or harder to kill, and the loot rolls.
  • Where it shows: uncorrupted T1–T3 maps and T4 dungeons. It's there to tell you whether a map is worth corrupting; once it's corrupted, its rolls are settled and the "at Level" line says what it's worth to you. Unique maps don't show it: their rolls never change.

Its color

The color of the tag's number says how the rolls compare with other maps of the same tier (the word Experience is always gold): gold is good, red better, purple the best. Every tier is cut about the same way among uncorrupted rare maps: about 1 in 6 shows no line, about half are gold, 1 in 4 red, and 1 in 12 purple (a little fewer red and purple in T1, more in T3). Most magic maps, about 4 in 5, show no line. T4 dungeons always show it: about 6 in 10 gold, 3 in 10 red, and 1 in 10 purple. Can't tell the colors apart? The “at Level” line: what the map is worth to you

This map with its rolls: the experience of a full clear, for you, divided by the distance you walk to clear it
The average non-magical T1–T3 map: the same, for you

1.0x is an average plain map. 1.1x is 10% more experience for the same distance walked. Below 1.0x, an average plain map would do better. T4 dungeons are compared with the average plain T3 map instead (T4 dungeons).

  • The map itself: how many monsters spawn, how many champion and unique packs, their level, and the bosses in its layout.
  • Its rolls: the same ones as in the Experience line.
  • The distance: every map has a clear path, the route that takes you close to 95% of the places its monsters spawn (most players clear 90–95% of a map), starting where you arrive and averaged over its layouts. A big, spread-out map takes longer to clear, so its experience counts for less.
  • Your level: monsters well below your level give less experience. More on that below.
  • Your own +% experience: the map's Experience roll adds to the bonus you already have; it doesn't multiply it. So it's worth less the more you have: with an Annihilus (+10%), a map's +15% gives you about 14% more, not 15%.

Its color

The color of the line's number says how far ahead of an average non-magical map this map is for you (the words "at Level" are always gold): gold, then red, then purple, the furthest ahead. On T1–T3 and unique maps the number alone decides it, so a 2.5x is purple at any level. T4 dungeons, compared with the T3 maps and far ahead of them, have cuts of their own, which rise from level 95. Can't tell the colors apart? Compare the number with this table.

Maps Gold Red Purple

Of rare T1–T3 maps, about 1 in 3 shows red or purple and 1 in 20 purple. Corrupted T2 and T3 maps, the ones most players run at high levels, are red or purple far more often: about two in three at level 94. Dungeons are made from T3 maps and can be corrupted like them, so their cuts give them about as much color as a T3 map at your level: an uncorrupted dungeon as often as an uncorrupted T3 map, a corrupted one as often as a corrupted T3 map (at level 94, about 1 in 4 uncorrupted rare dungeons shows red or purple, and two in three corrupted ones, 1 in 5 of them purple). A dungeon's line climbs from level 95, and its cuts climb with it: why.

    T4 dungeons

    The Horadric Cube makes a dungeon from a T3 map and a Horadrim Scarab (the map can be non-magical, magic, or rare), or from three dungeons. Every dungeon comes out rare, with six random affixes of its own. On top of those, the recipe gives every dungeon the same five preset affixes, each rolled within its range:

    • +40–50% Experience, added to any Experience its random affixes roll
    • Monsters have 20–30% increased Velocity
    • Monsters have +80–100% Attack Rating and Chance to Pierce
    • Monsters' melee attacks deal splash damage
    • Monsters Cannot Be Frozen and have 10–20% increased Maximum Life

    Only the Experience counts in the two lines: the other four make its monsters faster, harder hitting, and longer to kill, not worth more. A dungeon's affixes can't be rerolled, but it can be corrupted like a map. A dungeon has two floors, and its monsters are level 92: champions 94, uniques and their minions 95.

    ⚠

    Dangerous and deadly. Every dungeon monster has several immunities and hits hard, and a dungeon can take upwards of 45 minutes to an hour or longer. The "at Level" line counts experience for the distance you walk, not for the time you spend, and a dungeon's monsters take far longer to kill than a map's: its number doesn't tell you what a dungeon gives you an hour.

      What it leaves out

      • How fast you kill: monster life, damage, resistances, immunities, and your build. It assumes the time a clear takes grows with the distance you walk.
      • Loot: Magic Find and the drop rolls.
      • Random map events.
      • Everything between maps: time in town, loading screens, opening maps.

      Good to know

      • The lines show on identified maps only: an unidentified map has no rolls yet. The "Experience" line shows on uncorrupted T1–T3 maps and T4 dungeons only.
      • The "at Level" line shows for characters of level 80–98. At level 99 there's no experience to gain, so there's no line.
      • The tag is cut to two decimals, so its number always matches its color: 1.649 shows as 1.64x. The "at Level" line is rounded to two decimals: 1.155 shows as 1.16x, and 0.999 as 1.00x. Its color follows the number shown, so 1.996 is a red 2.00x.
      • They are estimates, worked out from how maps are built and how monsters spawn, not counted in game.

      How it's worked out

      • Monsters. Every area of a map spawns monsters in its rooms. Monster Density adds spawns, up to a limit each area has. Champion and unique packs fall between the area's own minimum and maximum, and Monster Rarity raises both. Fortified halves the spawns and doubles each monster's experience. A "Map contains ..." monster joins the area's own monsters as one more kind, picked for about 1 spawn in 10 (1 in 7 to 1 in 20, depending on the area). The bosses and monsters placed in a map's layout count too.
      • Experience per monster. It follows the monster's level: the map's area level, plus any Area Level roll (each level is worth about 2.5% more around level 87), 2 more for champions and 3 more for uniques and their minions.
      • Your level. Each kind of monster gets the level penalty above: 81%, 62%, 43%, 24%, and then 5% for a monster 6, 7, 8, 9, and 10 or more levels below you. Then your own +% experience and the map's are added together and applied.
      • Clear path. Measured on the map's own layouts, starting where you arrive (the map start on the wiki's layout pictures: on T1–T3 maps, the waypoint): walk to the nearest place a monster can spawn that you haven't come within 7 tiles of yet, then put those stops in the shortest order found and walk them again, leaving out the hardest-to-reach places, until you've come within 7 tiles of 95% of them. Averaged over every layout of the map, and over walks begun at the start and a few steps from it. Horazon's Memory's lightning spires are left out (players walk past them).
      • The 1.0x. The average of the 29 T1–T3 maps with no rolls: their average experience for you over their average clear path. For T4 dungeons, the average of the T3 maps.
      • T4 dungeons. The average rare dungeon's rolls come from rolling many dungeons the way the game does, six random affixes plus the five preset ones: on average +107% Monster Density, +10% Monster Rarity, and +65% Experience. The corrupted one takes each of the ten ways a corruption can go, one in ten each, on top of those: on average +41% Monster Density, +17% Monster Rarity, and +2% Experience more, and +1 Area Level 1 time in 10. Dungeons to your next level count full clears of both floors, the average of the three dungeons.

Season 14 · November 16th · v1 and v2

This update is about maps. Their highlights now follow how maps really roll, three warnings look at your own character, and the lines that rarely matter got quieter. Pick a tier and a kind of map in the bar: the charts and tables below follow it.

  • Density and rarity have their own cuts on each tier, and corrupted maps are measured against corrupted maps. Gold, red and purple each mean something on every kind of map: before, an uncorrupted map could hardly ever get past gold. See the cuts on rolled maps
  • Low Monster Density marks about 1 in 8 uncorrupted rare maps, the thinnest ones. Before, it marked about 2 in 5.
  • Chance to Block, Hit Recovery and All Resistances check the character hovering the map: how many more hits land on you, how many hit recovery frames you lose, how much resistance you lose once what's over the cap has soaked the map's minus. Try your character
  • Most other lines have new bands: small rolls no longer show, and red and purple are kept for the rolls that hurt. Unique maps count as corrupted, and get a tag line. Every line, before and now
  • Removed: the players' +X% Attack/Cast Speed and +X% Jewelry/Charms bonuses. Ghosts, Fetishes and Hell Bovines show on uncorrupted maps only.
  • v2, later the same day: Hit Recovery turns red from 2 frames lost and purple from 4, on every map.

Monster density and rarity

Density and rarity decide how many monsters and elite packs a map has, so they get the starred lines: +84% Monster Density, +40% Monster Rarity, or +40% Rarity +84% Density when a map has both, in the higher of their two bands. The stars tell the band: gold, red or purple.

Before, one set of cuts served every map. Density lit up from +70% on T1 maps (+80% on T2, +90% on T3), turned red from +120% (+130%, +140%) and purple from +180% (+190%, +200%); rarity from 30, 60 and 80 on every tier. Uncorrupted maps almost never roll +120% density, so on them the line was always gold: a third to a half of them got it, and Low Monster Density marked most of the rest. Corrupted maps, which roll far more, used the same cuts.

Now every tier has its own cuts, from where its maps' rolls fall, and corrupted maps have their own:

  • The gold cut is the same on corrupted maps, so corrupting a map never takes its density line away. Its band can go down, as the map is then measured against other corrupted maps.
  • On an uncorrupted rare map, a density line says the map is worth corrupting.
  • Rarity grows with the tier: its prefix rolls +30–40% on T1 maps, up to +40–50% on T2 and +50–60% on T3 maps, and a corruption can add more. So its cuts grow too.
  • Low Monster Density marks the thinnest fifth of uncorrupted rare maps, when they have no rarity line and no crafting materials bonus: about 1 map in 8.

Warnings that check your character

Less block, slower hit recovery and lower resistances can cost one character a lot and the next nothing. A character with block or resistance to spare loses little or none of it; one who just reaches a hit recovery breakpoint loses a frame to the smallest minus. So these three lines now work out what the map costs the character hovering it, from that character's own stats, and show only when it matters: red when it hurts, purple when it hurts a lot.

The filter reads your stats when you hover the map, so each map is judged for the character you're playing. It can't see your weapon or a wereform: hit recovery uses your class's own frames. Try your numbers below; the kind of map in the bar sets the bands for corrupted maps, and how big a minus the map can roll.

−X% Chance to Block

Your chance to block is (your class's bonus + your shield's block) × (dexterity − 15) ÷ (2 × level), at most 75%. The map's minus comes off the shield's block, before that step, and the line shows how many more of the monsters' hits get through. From 30% block to 7% is 70% of the hits landing to 93%: 33% more hits.

−X% Hit Recovery

Hit recovery works in breakpoints: what counts is how many frames a hit stuns you. The map's minus comes off your faster hit recovery, and the line shows the frames you lose: red from 2, purple from 4. One frame lost, or none, shows nothing. Below 0% faster hit recovery the frames climb fast (a Sorceress at −20% takes 31 frames to recover), and from −36% the game's hit recovery wraps around and you recover at once: that gets a gray line, on uncorrupted maps.

−X% All Resistances

The map's minus comes off your fire, cold and lightning resistance, and whatever you have over the cap soaks it first. The line shows the most resistance you lose of the three: red from 5 on uncorrupted maps and from 10 on every map, purple from 15. Fully covered, it shows nothing. A corrupted map's −X% Max Resistances lowers the cap: its own line warns of that, and your resistance sits that much further over the lower cap.

Every other line

Most other lines have new bands. Rolls too small to matter no longer show, and red and purple are kept for the rolls that hurt. Gray lines are details for deciding what to do with an uncorrupted map, so as before they show on uncorrupted maps only. Unique maps now count as corrupted: they show the red and purple lines alone.

  • Fire, Light, Cold and Poison Damage show only on maps whose monsters deal that element or get that extra damage; their shares are for those maps.
  • Extra Damage counts the kinds of extra damage the monsters get (lightning, cold, fire, poison, magic). Each comes from its own prefix and no corruption adds one, so a rolled rare map has three at most. A Treacherous map (cubed with a Corrupted Ear) can have one more: the Sorceress's ear adds lightning, the Druid's fire and the Necromancer's poison, unless the map has it already. So Quadruple Extra Damage (purple) shows only on those: about 1 rare one in 470 in T1, 1 in 330 in T2 and 1 in 270 in T3. Triple shows on 7 to 9 in 100 of them (under 1 in 100 without an ear). Dual doesn't show on Treacherous maps (nor on corrupted ones) any more: about 4 in 10 of them would have it.
  • Map contains: Ghosts, Fetishes and Hell Bovines (teal) now show on uncorrupted maps only, as Vampire Lords (gray) already did. Stygian Dolls, Burning Souls, Succubus Witches, Reanimated Horde and Minions of Destruction are as before.
  • Removed: the players' +X% Attack/Cast Speed and +X% Jewelry/Charms, two bonuses only a corruption gives.

The tag line

Under a map's name, the tag line sums up its rolls: experience, rarity, density and magic find, such as 11exp 62rar 64den 127mf. Its rar and den now take the colour of the rarity and density bands above (light gray below gold), with a corrupted map's den judged by the corrupted cuts. Magic find shows from 100 in yellow, or in light gray when the map has a density or rarity line or a crafting materials bonus; experience from 10, when the map has a density or rarity line.

The corrupted T3 map on the Examples page: its +62% rarity was in the red band before. On T3 maps, where rarity rolls higher, it is gold now.

Unique maps get a tag line too now, in plain colours: light gray den, rar and mf (mf yellow from 100) and orange exp. They get no density or rarity line. The experience lines under the highlights (1.8x Experience, 2.1x at Level 90) are unchanged: Map Experience explains them.

The Corrlorized versions

Corr's versions keep his own choices for maps: no density, rarity or tag lines, no Low Monster Density, and more than a dozen other lines off, All Resistances and Max Resistances among them. The lines they do show follow the new cuts, in his colours.

How the numbers on this page were made

The charts and shares come from 200,000 uncorrupted and 200,000 corrupted rare maps and 50,000 magic maps of each tier, rolled as the game rolls them with Season 14's map affixes and corruptions (the November 4th patch): a rare map's affixes, three prefixes and three suffixes at most, a magic map's prefix and suffix, and one corruption on the corrupted ones, every map of a tier as likely as the next. Unique maps and T4 dungeons aren't in them.

    Grail Planner

    The holy grail is finding every unique and set item in the game at least once: 555 of them. Mark the ones you have, and the planner shows you the fastest way to the rest: which maps to run, which Corrupted Ear to use and which bosses to farm.

    Never hidden until you've found it

    A unique or set item you haven't found yet is never hidden, on any filter level: it drops with orange stars around its name, a minimap icon of its own, a chat notification and a drop sound. Once you've found it, it looks as its value tier says.

              What the box reads and what each button does
              • Quickest: open the Compendium view and page through it beside the game's own Compendium, clicking the cards you've discovered.
              • Found most of it? Type what you're missing in the box, one name per line, and press "Mark all but these discovered": every other item is marked discovered. In game, the stash's Uniques and Sets tabs list what you're missing when you turn on "Show Undiscovered" (hover an item for its name). Or press "Mark all discovered" and mark the missing ones undiscovered one by one under Every Item: type a name in its search and press Enter.
              • Found a few? Type them in the box and press "Mark these discovered", or click their boxes under Every Item. For a whole set or base at once, search it there and press "Mark listed items discovered".
              • The box's buttons: "Mark these discovered" adds the names to your list, "Mark these undiscovered" takes them off, "Mark all but these discovered" marks every item discovered but the names, and "Replace my list" marks the names discovered and everything else undiscovered.
              • What the box reads: one item per line, from a spreadsheet, another grail tracker or typed by hand (Harlequin Crest, Arm of King Leoric). Capitals, apostrophes, a leading "The", bullets, numbering and a checked box such as [x] don't matter, a version in brackets counts as the item (Harlequin Crest (Shako)), and a spreadsheet row counts by the cell with the name. Lines with an empty box ([ ], ✗) and rows marked no, false, 0 or ✗ are skipped, and the lines it doesn't recognize are listed under the buttons (the first 10). Items that don't count for the grail (How It's Worked Out names them) are listed apart.
              • Keep or move your list: "Copy" copies the list of your discovered items ("Show my list" shows it in the box). On another browser or device, paste it into this box and press "Replace my list".
              • Undo takes back your changes one at a time, latest first, while you stay on the page.

                Unique Map Bosses

                The bosses of four unique maps drop extra loot when they die, on top of their own: three drop random uniques, Djinn's Domain's two Djinns a treasure explosion each.                   T4 Dungeons

                  Every monster killed in a T4 dungeon drops its loot twice, and its two bosses, one on its first floor and one on its last, each set off a treasure explosion when they die. But dungeons are dangerous and deadly: every monster has several immunities, few builds can clear one, and a dungeon can take upwards of 45 minutes to an hour or longer. Here is what a whole dungeon finds, and its two bosses alone, against T1-T3 maps in the same time.

                                    How It's Worked Out

                                    • The grail: every unique and set item in the game, unique maps, quest items and nine uniques that don't count for the grail left out (Itherael's Path, Overlord's Helm, Dark Abyss, Aidan's Scar, Hadriel's Hand, Band of Skulls, Cage of the Unsullied, The Third Eye and Rainbow Facet). An item that comes in several versions (Akarat's Devotion, Cyclopean Roar and Arm of King Leoric, two versions each) is one grail item: any of its versions finds it, so its chances are those of all its versions together.
                                    • Drops: each monster's drops and their quality as the game rolls them: each base's chance to be unique or set at the monster's level (class items have their own, better chances), magic find with its diminishing returns, and a unique picked among the base's uniques by their weight. A unique roll on a base with no unique becomes a rare, a set roll with no set item magic.
                                    • Maps: full clears, averaged over the maps of a tier, from the map model behind the filter's experience tags (monsters per area, elite packs, monster levels; the website's Map Experience page explains it). "Rare" is the average rare map with a corruption: its density, monster rarity and magic find. Solo; one map boss per map, each map's own: its own loot at its own level (80 to 91; map bosses keep their level as other bosses do).
                                    • The Bitter Depths: the map event in about 1 rare T1-T3 map in 26 and 1 non-magical map in 82 (its portal always opens, late in the clear). Its 20 Lucion's chests drop as a tier-0 vault's do, 5 items each; its monsters (5 elite packs) and Zoltun Kulle drop as map monsters do, and your ear works on them, not on the chests. The map's magic find carries into it, for the chests too. Zoltun's death also sets off a treasure explosion: his loot again and again until 60 items lie on the ground, at his level, with no magic find (your ear doesn't touch it either). Its 7 sparkly chests are left out (about 3% of its chests' grail drops). Every map's numbers include it at that chance.
                                    • Cow King's Leathers: only a Hell Bovine's drop can be one of its pieces: the cows of the Secret Cow Level and the Cow King. Blood Moon's cows are other monsters and never drop it. Maps have Hell Bovines two ways, and the page counts both for these three items: the Bovine guest ("Map contains Hell Bovines"), a prefix on about 1 rare T1 map in 18 and 1 rare T2 or T3 map in 28, which adds the cows to every area's monsters (in groups and elite packs, beside the map's own) with +25-30% density and +15-20% magic find; and the Dark Wanderer map event, whose three wanderers each turn into a random act boss or superunique: the Cow King, with his 8 Hell Bovines, in about 1 rare map in 260 and 1 non-magical map in 800 (an event always brings all three).
                                    • Grail progress: each item drops at random at its own rate, so the items found after n maps add up item by item. "Switching" works from the expected progress; with your real list the planner does better.
                                    • Farm spots: one kill of each act boss and superunique in each difficulty, solo: the monster's own drop at its own level. The drops of the minions around a superunique are counted apart, as "with its minions". In Normal difficulty a monster has a fixed level of its own; in Nightmare and Hell it takes the area's level, but bosses keep theirs; superuniques and their minions are 3 levels higher. A kill makes at most six items, gold and potions included, so Duriel's many drop rolls always end at six. A boss drops its quest loot only while the quest is open; farming kills drop the usual loot.
                                    • Spots: "fixed" means the monster stands in the same place every game: its area is one of the game's preset maps (the Cathedral, Tower Cellar Level 5, Nihlathak's Temple...), or laid out the same way every game (Travincal, the Chaos Sanctuary, the Bloody Foothills), or it waits in a piece the game always puts at the same edge of its area (Eldritch by the Bloody Foothills, Stormtree in the Flayer Jungle at its border with Lower Kurast). The others stand in a piece of the area the game places anywhere in it. "Areas from a waypoint" counts the areas to walk through from the nearest waypoint (towns have one; portals count as one step).
                                    • Corrupted Zones: in Hell games made with Baal's quest done, one group of areas at a time has area level 85, so its superuniques are level 88 (bosses keep their own level).
                                    • Ubers and events:
                                      • Lilith, Uber Duriel and Uber Izual drop their own loot at level 110.
                                      • Diablo Clone drops Annihilus every kill; Uber Tristram drops the Hellfire Torch once a run, when its last boss dies (the three bosses drop nothing else).
                                      • The Uber Ancients: before touching the altar you switch on the ancients you want to fight at their statues, one, two or all three; the altar then drops 20 to 29 items for one ancient, 32 to 47 for two and 44 to 65 for three, at item level 90.
                                      • Lucion's vault has 10 chests at every tier (0, 1 and 2, easiest first); each drops 3 items, then 2, 3 or 4 more from better loot on tier 0, 1 or 2, so a harder tier gives more items, not better ones. The items have the vault's level (89); the level of the character who opens the chest (95 here) goes into their quality. The numbers are for the whole vault; Lucion himself drops no grail items.
                                      • The vault's key is cubed from three parts, each a chance per kill in Hell (Lucion's Chests names them; solo, as every number here).
                                    • Unique map bosses: the bosses of four unique maps drop extra loot when they die, on top of their own. Unique maps open only in Hell, one a game; one run a map, and the rest of the map isn't counted.
                                      • Three drop random uniques: Gregus Mazi in City of Ureh 3 (socketed), Warlord of Blood 2 or 3 (corrupted) and Na-Krul in Na-Krul's Abyss 3 (ethereal, but for the indestructible ones).
                                      • Each starts as a unique picked at random, every unique alike but the few with no rarity (Embersworn among them; Rainbow Facets are in; Gregus Mazi passes over bases that take no sockets). Na-Krul drops the unique he picked.
                                      • Warlord of Blood and Gregus Mazi keep only its base and make a unique of it at item level 99, picked as any dropped unique is, by how common each unique of that base is (one already dropped in the game comes out rare).
                                      • Their own loot drops as a map boss's does, at their level (91), with the map's own magic find.
                                      • Djinn's Domain's two Djinns, Siren of the Forgotten Vault and Djinn of a Thousand Blades, each set off a treasure explosion when they die: their own loot again and again until 60 items lie on the ground, at their level (90), with no magic find. Their own loot drops too, twice (Djinn's Domain repeats every monster's drop) and a third time half the time (the map's extra drop).
                                      • The minibosses (Zayl the Merciful in City of Ureh, Cory the Imp and Onion the Accused in Warlord of Blood) drop only their own loot, as their map's boss does (level 91). A run counts it: players kill them too.
                                    • T4 dungeons: one run a dungeon, a whole clear of its two floors, the average of the three dungeons. The cube makes them rare: an average rare dungeon with a corruption (+148% density, +27% monster rarity, and +161% magic find on top of yours).
                                      • Every monster killed in a T4 dungeon drops its own loot twice, the bosses too; a unique monster's minions drop theirs once.
                                      • Its two bosses, one on its first floor and one on its last, each set off a treasure explosion when they die: the first floor's boss until 30 items lie on the ground, the last floor's until 60, at the boss's level (92; Skullsplitter Cyrus 90), with no magic find. Their loot, explosions and own alike, is the Uber Ancients' altar's. The three dungeons' bosses drop alike.
                                      • Dungeons are dangerous and deadly: every monster has several immunities, so few builds can clear one, and a dungeon can take upwards of 45 minutes to an hour or longer. The page weighs a dungeon against the full map clears 45 minutes to an hour holds (15 minutes each, the estimated time of Map Event and Boss Rushing).
                                    • Best bet: an item's best bet compares one kill with one map: target farming its best boss or superunique (any difficulty; Corrupted Zones, ubers, events, unique map bosses and T4 dungeon bosses left out) when one kill there finds it at least as often as a whole map with the item's best ear, maps otherwise. That's as if a kill took as long as a map; a quicker kill makes farming better still. Your Grail's plan sorts your missing items by it, and splits the maps' ones by their chance in your next 100 maps with the ear it names: an even chance or better, or the slowest.
                                    • Gheed's map event (its tab): each of his 5 offers is a random unique: any unique but jewels and the few with no rarity (Embersworn among them). One that needs character level 60 or more is turned down (level − 49)% of the time and another picked; in one shop in 200, one offer is a special ring instead.
                                    • Gambling: each gambled item's item level is your character level −5 to +4. The base becomes exceptional with a chance that grows 0.9% per item level above the exceptional version's level, else elite with 0.33% per level above the elite version's (only once the exceptional chance is above zero). Then it is unique 1 time in 2,000 and a set item 1 time in 1,000, picked among the uniques or sets of that base whose level is at most the item level, like a drop; with none, the roll is lost. Magic find plays no part. The gold: rings 50,000, amulets 63,000; any other base costs more the higher your character level, and the more so the higher its exceptional and elite versions' prices (no reduced vendor prices here). Cow King's Leathers never comes from gambling. Gambling ranks the bases.
                                    • Magic find: the bar is your character's magic find. Unique, set and rare chances grow with it, ever slower (the game's diminishing returns). A map's own magic find adds to yours before them, as the game does: ; the unique maps add their own.
                                    • Left out:
                                      • The rest of a unique map (only its bosses count).
                                      • The map events but the Bitter Depths (Treasure Fallen, Corrupted Heroes, Horazon, the Dark Wanderer and the rest; Gheed's shop has its own tab), and map guests. Cow King's Leathers alone counts the Bovine guest and the Dark Wanderer's Cow King. The website's Map Event and Boss Rushing page counts every event.
                                      • A rare map's extra weapon, armor and crafting drops (some of its suffixes).
                                      • Magic maps, Fortified maps and parties (the notes on the Corrupted Ears tab say what the last two change).
                                      • Whole area clears: a full Cow Level, Chaos Sanctuary or Baal run also drops from every monster on the way, so those runs find more than the boss alone shows here.
                                      • PD2's special drops (sigils, Corrupted Zone shards) aren't grail items.
                                    Words on this page
                                    1 in 1,200 mapsA chance, not a promise: on average one drop every 1,200 full map clears. A 90% chance to see the item takes about 2.3 times that many; luck runs ahead or behind.
                                    Kills, runs, maps, dungeons, vaults, shops, gamblesFarm spots count per kill; per run for Uber Tristram and the Uber Ancients (one, two or three ancients, your choice at their statues); per map for the unique map bosses (one run of the map); per dungeon for a T4 dungeon (a whole one, or its two bosses); per vault (its 10 chests) for Lucion's vault; per shop for Gheed's map event; per gamble (with its gold) for gambling; per run for the Bitter Depths (its chests, monsters and Zoltun Kulle), which every map's numbers count at the event's chance. A kill and a map clear take very different times, so compare spots with spots and maps with maps.
                                    No ear, Any ear, Best earAn item's chance per map without a Corrupted Ear, with an ear whose pool doesn't have the item's base (what every ear gives), and with the ear whose pool has it.
                                    Best ear ×How many times more often the best ear finds an item than no ear.
                                    Maps for 90%The maps for a 90% chance to see an item.
                                    Best betWhere an item comes from best: target farming its best boss or superunique when one kill there finds it at least as often as a whole map with its best ear ("beats maps"), maps otherwise.
                                    A kill = 3 mapsOne kill finds the item as often as 3 maps with its best ear: farming wins as long as a kill takes you less time than 3 maps.
                                    Drop levelThe lowest monster level that can drop an item: not the level you need to wear it. The farm spots reach level 68 in Normal and 75 in Nightmare, so the highest-level items only come from Hell.
                                    Normal, Nightmare, HellThe difficulties. A "normal base" (beside exceptional and elite) is an item's base tier.
                                    Fixed spot, random spotA fixed spot is the same every game (the Cathedral, Travincal, Nihlathak's Temple); a random one is anywhere in its area.
                                    Areas from a waypointThe areas to walk through from the nearest waypoint (towns have one; a portal counts as one step).
                                    With its minionsA superunique's own drop is the main number; "with its minions" adds the monsters around it.
                                    Corrupted ZoneIn Hell games made with Baal's quest done, one group of areas at a time has area level 85, so its superuniques drop at level 88 while the zone lasts.
                                    Ubers and eventsLilith, Uber Duriel, Uber Izual, Diablo Clone, Uber Tristram, the Uber Ancients and Lucion's vault.
                                    A modelThe numbers follow how the game rolls drops (what each monster can drop, its quality, magic find) and the map model behind the filter's experience tags, solo: estimates, not a drop log.
